About the data

Home value is the Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI) for September 2026: the typical value of homes in the ZIP code (single-family homes and condos, the middle third of values, i.e. the 35th to 65th percentile, smoothed and seasonally adjusted). It is a model estimate of what homes are worth, not a sale price or a median. The change is against September 2025.

Monthly rent is the Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI) for September 2026 where Zillow publishes one (8,333 ZIP codes, mostly cities and suburbs): the typical asking rent for listed homes and apartments, smoothed. Where Zillow has no rent, the map shows (striped) the Census Bureau's median gross rent from the American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, table B25064: what renters actually paid, averaged over 2020–2024, including utilities. Census rents run well below Zillow asking rents because they include long-time tenants, so compare striped ZIPs with each other, not with solid ones. Census caps its figure at $3,500 ("$3,500 or more"). Each Census figure has a margin of error (90%), shown on hover.

Income is median household income (table B19013: the yearly income of all household members, before taxes, in 2024 dollars), American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, for ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, tracts and block groups. Census caps it at $250,000.

Race is table B03002 (Hispanic or Latino origin by race) for 2020 block groups, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates. Each block group's counts become dots (one per 20 people, rounded at random so totals stay right on average), placed at random inside its land area; zoomed out, a random 1 in 5 or 1 in 25 of those dots is drawn. These are survey estimates with margins of error, and block groups are larger than the census blocks a decennial-census dot map uses, so dots in rural areas are spread over wide areas.

Block groups (the third area choice) are subdivisions of tracts, usually 600 to 3,000 people; values, rents and incomes come from the same Census tables as tracts, with wider margins of error. Zoomed out, tracts are shown.

Census tracts (the second area choice) show median home value (table B25077: owners' own estimates of what their home would sell for) and median gross rent (B25064), American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, with 90% margins of error on hover, on 2022 cartographic tract boundaries (2020 tracts). They are a different source and time period from the Zillow ZIP figures and run lower. Census caps values at $2,000,000 and rents at $3,500.

Areas are the Census Bureau's 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, which approximate ZIP codes; PO-box-only ZIPs have no area. Zillow ZIPs are matched by their 5-digit code (7 Zillow value ZIPs and 4 rent ZIPs have no 2020 area and are left out). Grey areas have no estimate.

Street map (this website version): OpenFreeMap's Liberty style, built from OpenStreetMap data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, OpenMapTiles). The colors sit on it see-through, below its roads and names; the color-strength slider also fades the roads, so stronger colors mean fainter roads. The text below describes the simpler background used when the map runs inside claude.ai.

Background map: land is the Census Bureau's 2020 state boundaries (1:500,000, clipped to the shoreline); lakes and rivers are Natural Earth 1:10m (public domain); highways and major roads are Census TIGER roads (interstates, US and state highways, county roads), simplified. It shows major roads only, not every street. Town names are Zillow's city list placed with the Census 2024 Gazetteer.

Data downloaded November 10, 2026: Zillow Research public data (files.zillowstatic.com), Census ACS summary file (www2.census.gov), 2020 cartographic boundaries and the 2024 Gazetteer of places. Zillow data © Zillow, used under its research data terms.
