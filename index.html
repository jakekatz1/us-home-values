About the data

Home value is the Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI) for September 2026: the typical value of homes in the ZIP code (single-family homes and condos, the middle third of values, i.e. the 35th to 65th percentile, smoothed and seasonally adjusted). It is a model estimate of what homes are worth, not a sale price or a median. The change is against September 2025.

Monthly rent is the Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI) for September 2026 where Zillow publishes one (8,333 ZIP codes, mostly cities and suburbs): the typical asking rent for listed homes and apartments, smoothed. Where Zillow has no rent, the map shows (striped) the Census Bureau's median gross rent from the American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, table B25064: what renters actually paid, averaged over 2020–2024, including utilities. Census rents run well below Zillow asking rents because they include long-time tenants, so compare striped ZIPs with each other, not with solid ones. Census caps its figure at $3,500 ("$3,500 or more"). Each Census figure has a margin of error (90%), shown on hover.

Income is median household income (table B19013: the yearly income of all household members, before taxes, in 2024 dollars), American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, for ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, tracts and block groups. Census caps it at $250,000.

Race is the 2020 Census (redistricting data, table P2: Hispanic or Latino, and not Hispanic by race) for every census block, the same source censusdots.com uses. Zoomed in, the page draws one dot per person (then one per 4, one per 20 as you zoom out), each placed at random within its block's footprint; in cities a block is one street block, in the country it can be several square miles. Zoomed out, ready-made dots stand for 100 or 500 people each. Counts are full 2020 counts, so they don't show change since then.

Age of homes is table B25035 (median year structure built), American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates: the year by which half of an area's housing units had been built, as reported by residents. 1939 or earlier is the survey's oldest category. It covers homes only and is an area median; there is no national source of build years for individual buildings.

Commute is table B08301 (means of transportation to work), American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates: how workers aged 16 and over usually got to work in the survey week, as a share of all workers. Transit includes bus, subway, rail and ferry; walk, bike combines walking and bicycling; taxi, motorcycle and other means show on hover as Other. Areas with fewer than 20 workers are grey.

Crime is offenses known to law enforcement in 2025 from the FBI's Crime Data Explorer (Crime in the United States 2025, tables 8 and 10), for agencies that reported all twelve months. Violent crime is murder and nonnegligent manslaughter, rape, robbery and aggravated assault; property crime is burglary, larceny-theft and motor vehicle theft. Each city police department covers its Census city (or town or township) boundary, with the FBI's population for the rate; each county sheriff or county police department covers its county outside those city areas, with residents estimated from Census block groups. Cities that reported only part of the year are grey, as are county areas with no reporting county agency (state police, campus, transit and tribal agencies are not mapped). A city force that also patrols its county (Las Vegas Metropolitan, Charlotte-Mecklenburg) covers both. 66 city departments (1.0 million people), mostly regional departments that serve several towns, could not be matched to a boundary and are left out. These are crimes reported to police: reporting rates differ by crime and place, and per-resident rates run high wherever many visitors, workers or shoppers come in.

Block groups (the finest area choice) are subdivisions of tracts, usually 600 to 3,000 people; values, rents and incomes come from the same Census tables as tracts, with wider margins of error. Zoomed out, tracts are shown.

Census tracts show median home value (table B25077: owners' own estimates of what their home would sell for) and median gross rent (B25064), American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, with 90% margins of error on hover, on 2022 cartographic tract boundaries (2020 tracts). They are a different source and time period from the Zillow ZIP figures and run lower. Census caps values at $2,000,000 and rents at $3,500.

ZIP codes are the Census Bureau's 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, which approximate ZIP codes; PO-box-only ZIPs have no area. Zillow ZIPs are matched by their 5-digit code (7 Zillow value ZIPs and 4 rent ZIPs have no 2020 area and are left out). Grey areas have no estimate.

Street map: OpenFreeMap's Liberty style, built from OpenStreetMap data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, OpenMapTiles). The colors sit on it see-through, below its roads and names; the color-strength slider also fades the roads, so stronger colors mean fainter roads.

Data downloaded November 12, 2026: Zillow Research public data (files.zillowstatic.com), Census ACS summary file and 2020 Census redistricting files (www2.census.gov), FBI Crime Data Explorer, 2020 cartographic boundaries and the 2024 Gazetteer of places.
